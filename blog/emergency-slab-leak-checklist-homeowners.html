What to Do When You Suspect a Slab Leak Right Now

Published: June 18, 2026 • Reading Time: 4 min read

A slab leak is a true residential emergency. The moment you notice hot floor spots, damp baseboards, or a water meter spinning rapidly, you must act quickly to prevent erosion and minimize damage. Having an emergency response plan can save your home from costly foundation settling.

Step 1: Shut Off the Main Water Line immediately. Locate your home's main interior shutoff valve and turn it clockwise to stop pressurized water from flowing through your plumbing system. If you cannot find the interior valve, open the water meter box at your curb near the street and use a curb key to shut off the meter valve.

Step 2: Shut Off Your Water Heater. If the leak is on the hot water line, your water heater will run continuously, trying to heat incoming cold water. This can cause the unit to overheat or burnout. Turn off the electrical breaker (for electric heaters) or turn the gas control knob to "Pilot" (for gas units).

Step 3: Clear the Area & Document. Move furniture, rugs, and electrical devices away from damp spots on the flooring. Take clear photos and videos of the standing water, warped baseboards, and water meter dial for your insurance records.

Step 4: Contact a Certified Slab Leak Specialist. Call a leak detection company immediately. Do not attempt to hire general handymen who do not possess specialized acoustic sensors or tracer gas diagnostic equipment, as they may suggest costly, blind concrete demolition.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is my home's main water shutoff valve in Owensboro?

Most valves are located where the main line enters the home (utility closet, crawl space entrance, or basement wall) or outside at the water meter box near the street curb.

Should I turn off my water heater during a slab leak?

Yes. If the leak is on the hot water line, leaving the heater running can burn out the heating elements or cause pressure build-up. Turn off the breaker or gas valve.
